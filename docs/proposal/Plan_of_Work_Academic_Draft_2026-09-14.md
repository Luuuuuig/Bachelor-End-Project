# Plan of Work academic sections

Yijie Wang | Hytech-Pommec | TU/e OPAC | 14 September 2026

## Project context and problem

Hytech-Pommec develops and manufactures hyperbaric and life-support systems. Purchasing supports the supply of components and services for its products and projects. The operational buyer uses Exact to process purchasing requirements, prepare purchase orders, allocate purchased quantities to underlying demand, and record supplier information.

Initial process mapping and observations show several forms of manual work. The buyer transfers request information into Exact, checks purchasing details, compares prices and supplier confirmations, and seeks clarification when information is incomplete or uncertain. Order preparation also involves judgement about urgency and opportunities to combine demand for the same supplier. These observations describe the current work; they do not yet establish its largest workload contributors, root causes or achievable savings.

The practical problem is that purchasing requires operational-buyer effort whose distribution and causes have not yet been established sufficiently to select an improvement. The research therefore first develops an evidence-based account of the work. It then selects a bounded task or coherent process component for an artificial intelligence (AI) supported intervention and evaluates its effect.

## Objective and research questions

The objective is to reduce the operational buyer's workload within the defined purchasing scope while maintaining the required quality of purchasing outcomes. Relevant tactical purchasing work is included to understand tasks, information flows and responsibilities that may affect operational purchasing. Logistics remains outside the thesis, following the academic decision of 10 September 2026.

The approved main research question is retained:

> To what extent can an AI-supported solution reduce the operational buyers' workload at Hytech-Pommec without reducing the quality of the purchasing outcome?

The approved sub-questions are retained in their existing order:

1. Which parts of the operational purchasing workflow contribute most to the operational buyer's workload in terms of frequency, processing time, rework and judgement required?
2. What conditions and control measures are required for the proposed solution to be implemented reliably in the operational purchasing workflow?
3. Which of these activities offers the greatest potential for AI-supported improvement, considering workload contribution, business relevance, technical feasibility and the need for human expertise?
4. To what extent does the proposed AI-supported solution reduce workload while maintaining the required quality of the purchasing activity, when compared with current practice?

The empirical workload baseline concerns Arno. Findings about his observed work will not be presented as estimates for all buyers. Dennis's evidence provides an additional purchasing perspective and remains identifiable by actor.

Relevant corrections within included tasks remain visible where the source supports them and the scope rules permit them.

Recommendations will cover the selected improvement's use and monitoring and other supported purchasing opportunities. The project retains the meaningful AI contribution agreed on 3 September. A specific technical solution, autonomous decision authority and production integration have not been selected.

## Research design

Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve and Control (DMAIC) provides the overarching process-improvement structure, following de Mast and Lokkerbol (2012). Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) guides artifact objectives, development, demonstration and evaluation within Improve, following Peffers et al. (2007). The exploratory baseline informs case selection; the later intervention comparison addresses the effect of the selected solution.

### Define and process discovery

The current AS-IS map, task register, purchasing-document review and buyer walkthrough provide the operational starting point. Targeted walkthroughs and observations with Dennis will establish the tactical context and purchasing handoffs. A high-level thesis view will include the relevant purchasing stages and end before logistics. Any broader company view remains separate from the thesis evidence boundary.

Van Weele's purchasing-process stages organize the process context. The model distinguishes specification, supplier selection, contracting, ordering, monitoring and evaluation, as discussed by Bäckstrand et al. (2019). The local work families remain observation codes. A separate post-session stage field locates the purpose of each evidenced task. Mapping by purpose allows, for example, CHECK to represent either a pre-order price check or a supplier-confirmation check without treating the two as the same process stage.

### Measure

Structured observation records work episodes, reliable start and end times, meaningful volume, interruptions, decision evidence and short contextual notes. The method separates case origin and channel from the activity performed. Request intake is timed when intake itself is visible work. A genuinely standalone instantaneous decision is tallied; decisions embedded in timed work remain attributes of that episode. No duration is inferred for unobserved cognition or untimed actions.

Workload includes the amount and difficulty of work, following Bowling and Kirkendall (2012). The study reports observable indicators separately. Processing time does not directly measure mental workload, for which Young et al. (2015) provides the distinct conceptual reference. The observation design uses time-and-motion principles, with explicit recording of coverage and task transitions, consistent with Zheng et al. (2011). The purchasing codes are project-specific and do not inherit validation from those sources.

| Evidence | Analytical use | Unit and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Task occurrence | Describe which included work recurs | Episodes or events per documented exposure hour, separated by actor. |
| Active handling time | Describe effort within included tasks | Reliable minutes per comparable episode; aggregate linked episodes only where case coverage supports it. |
| Processed volume | Interpret task size | Relevant lines or items, distinguishing added lines from total order size. |
| Interruptions and clarification | Describe work conditions and investigation | Occurrences and supported episode time within the stated scope. |
| Decision and difficulty notes | Identify judgement and information needs | Qualitative evidence and decision tallies; no inferred pure decision duration. |
| Van Weele stage | Locate work in the purchasing process | One supported stage, MULTI or UNKNOWN; no duplicated minutes. |

Original records remain unchanged. Derived records retain source date, actor and OBS identifier, and document phase assignments, scope exclusions and uncertainty. EXC and logistics-related work are excluded consistently from the thesis profile. Ambiguous scope or phase remains flagged for review. System and supplier events contribute buyer time only when an active buyer episode is observed. Historical exposure totals and scope-specific totals are reported separately.

Five operational observation days form the initial sufficiency checkpoint. Coverage, cumulative patterns and new recurring activities determine whether targeted extension is needed. Dennis's dataset need not be equally large, but must support interpretable patterns. The review will document missing weekdays, dayparts, work families and case types. It will compare successive cumulative summaries and identify whether new information changes the interpretation. Completion of a scheduled observation window alone does not close Measure.

### Analyze and select the focal component

Analysis will identify supported workload problems within scope and trace their potential causes using the process map, concrete cases, purchasing records and targeted follow-up. A proposed cause will remain a hypothesis until the evidence supports it. An upstream task may be a suitable intervention point even when its effect is measured in Arno's work.

The initial operational candidates are order timing and consolidation, purchase-price and confirmation checks, request intake and clarification, PO communication, and a coherent approach to standard and non-standard purchasing cases. These are unranked candidates. Dennis's evidence may refine them or identify an additional purchasing component with a demonstrated connection to the objective. EXC and logistics will not enter the candidate set.

Before scoring, candidates must pass the existing permission and data-access, ground-truth, risk-control, study-time and meaningful-AI-fit gates. Remaining candidates will be compared using evidence-based criteria for workload contribution, business relevance, data readiness, AI suitability, feasibility and evaluation. Criteria, score anchors and weights will be agreed with the academic supervisor before aggregate ranking. Johan will validate the operational evidence and business feasibility. Sensitivity analysis will show whether reasonable changes in weights or uncertain scores change the preferred candidate.

Detailed Exact and Orbis feasibility checks follow candidate shortlisting. One component will be selected only when its evidence, access requirements and evaluation can be supported within the study period. Other opportunities remain recommendations. If the leading candidate fails a feasibility gate, another eligible AI-supported candidate will be considered through the same procedure.

### Improve and evaluate

DSRM will guide the translation of the selected problem into artifact requirements, design, demonstration and evaluation. The design may combine AI with purchasing-process changes or conventional automation. The intended AI contribution must address an evidenced task need. The project will establish the required level of human review and approval with the process owner for the chosen use case.

Before development and formal testing, the evaluation protocol will define eligible cases, the current-practice comparator, the primary workload endpoint, a meaningful improvement threshold and an activity-specific quality rubric. Active human handling time per comparable case or line is the current endpoint direction. It will include preparation, review, correction and rework attributable to the intervention. Purchasing quality will be assessed against the relevant task requirements. A workload improvement cannot compensate for failure of the quality requirement.

The comparison will use cases appropriate to the selected task and keep development material separate from formal evaluation cases. Where repeatability permits, comparable or matched cases and balanced condition order will help distinguish an intervention effect from case difficulty and learning. The final design, sample size and statistical analysis will follow the case characteristics and feasible observations. Results will report uncertainty, exclusions, AI cost and any work transferred to another purchasing role. A negative or no-benefit finding remains a valid result.

### Control

The final recommendations will define ownership, instructions for use, review or escalation arrangements, and measures for monitoring the selected workflow. They will distinguish demonstrated results from benefits that require later implementation evidence. Company-dependent evaluation and access work must fit before the 7 January 2027 placement boundary.
