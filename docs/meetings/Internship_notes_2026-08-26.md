## Work completed

The main work on this day was to strengthen the theoretical and practical basis of the Measure phase before piloting live observations.

- Refined the exploratory Measure-phase observation protocol for manual shadowing of the operational buyer.
- Kept the live-observation sheet deliberately compact so that data can be captured without losing track of the buyer's work.
- Maintained the distinction between TIME, TALLY, TIME IF and SYSTEM activities.
- Clarified that active processing time is useful for observable execution/verification work, but fast cognitive decisions should not be assigned artificial standalone durations.
- Reviewed possible validity issues in the draft protocol, including interruption capture, rework denominators, small timing samples, missed observations and the distinction between judgement and expertise dependence.
- Decided not to make all protocol refinements immediately; the priority is to pilot the method and then freeze only rules that are workable in practice.

## Literature and workload-definition work

A substantial part of the day was spent finding and organizing literature that better supports the project's broader workload construct.

The literature structure was refined as follows:

- **Bowling & Kirkendall (2012):** broad occupational workload framing, especially amount of work versus difficulty of work.
- **Spector & Jex (1998):** quantitative workload and organizational constraints.
- **Young et al. (2015):** mental workload specifically, rather than the definition of all operational-buyer workload.
- **ISO 9000:** generic quality anchor based on fulfilment of relevant requirements.

The resulting working principle is that the BEP should report a **multidimensional workload profile** rather than inventing an unsupported equation that combines processing time, interruptions, mental demand and rework into one total workload score.

Expertise dependence is also kept analytically separate from mental workload because fast recognition based on experience may be important without necessarily implying high mental workload.
