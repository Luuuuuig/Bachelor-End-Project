## 3. **Key Discussion Points**

### Research question and sub-questions

The supervisor stated that the main research question should be more specific. The wording around **“identifying opportunities”** was considered too vague because the project ultimately needs to answer a question that is directly linked to the objective of reducing workload.

The supervisor suggested revising the main research question so that it points directly to the project objective rather than only asking which opportunities exist.

The existing sub-questions were considered useful, but the recommended sequence is:

`SQ1 → SQ5 → SQ2 → SQ4`

**SQ3 should be deleted.**

### Possible later case-study / evaluation cases

For a later case-based evaluation, the supervisor suggested that different types of purchasing cases could be useful, for example:

- a straightforward, complete case/PO that can be processed or forwarded directly;
- an ambiguous case;
- a borderline or approximately “50/50” case.

No final case-study design was selected during this meeting.

### DMAIC and DSRM

The supervisor had not reviewed the methodology section in detail, but agreed with using **DMAIC**.

The student explained the intended methodological structure as:

- DMAIC is used to structure the broader process-improvement project;
- after Define, Measure and Analyze, DSRM will mainly support the Improve phase when a new artifact is designed and evaluated.

The supervisor indicated that this combination is acceptable as long as the thesis clearly explains the relationship between the project activities and the selected methodologies.

### Possible solution directions and price control

The supervisor initially considered the idea of providing the operational buyer with a small suggestion box or support interface while working in Exact. During the discussion, this particular idea was considered not practically feasible.

**Price control** was discussed as a direction worth investigating further. Both student and supervisor agreed that it could be promising, but the eventual improvement would need to be demonstrated quantitatively.

This means that if price control becomes the selected use case, the project must define a measurable before/after outcome showing how much the intervention improves performance.

The existing GitHub candidate portfolio was not reviewed during the meeting because the student could not locate it at that moment.

### Standard versus non-standard cases and AI support

The student presented the idea of first identifying and defining **standard purchasing cases**, then potentially supporting or processing those standard cases with an AI-based solution while allowing the operational buyer to focus on non-standard cases.

The supervisor's main feedback was that any such direction must still lead to a **quantitative evaluation of improvement**.

The student proposed a possible before/after design:

- conduct approximately **five working days** of baseline observation and measurement;
- determine how many cases the buyer processes and establish relevant workload/performance measures;
- after implementation, conduct another approximately **five-day measurement period**;
- compare average cases processed per day and other relevant measures before and after AI support.

This was discussed as a student-proposed evaluation approach and was **not yet finalized** as the formal evaluation design.

### Human decision authority

The supervisor noted that the current project contains an assumption that the final solution should still leave the final decision with a human.

The student explained that this assumption comes from observations suggesting that the organization may not want fully autonomous AI decision-making and may prefer to retain a certain level of human decision authority.

This remains an assumption that still requires validation and should not yet be treated as a confirmed design requirement.

### Workload definition and measurement

Young et al. was discussed as the basis for the workload construct.

The supervisor emphasized that the project should not only define workload theoretically, but should also be able to explain **how the relevant dimensions from Young et al. will actually be measured**.

The discussion led to the conclusion that the workload definition needs to be clearly established before the Measure phase is operationalized. The next step is therefore to first define the workload construct, then determine how the relevant dimensions will be measured, and only then begin structured measurement.
