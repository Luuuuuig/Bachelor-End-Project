### Purpose of the purchasing framework

**Recording: 10:47–17:50**

- Yijie asked how to handle activities that fit several stages, do not clearly fit a stage, or lack enough detail in the original notes to assign confidently.
- Hu explained that the framework gives the thesis an established basis for organising the work rather than creating an unsupported structure.
- Activities can be grouped by purchasing stage, and the time spent in each stage can be expressed as a share of total work time.
- Yijie reported that ordering accounted for a substantially larger share than the other stages in his preliminary view.
- Hu explained that a clearly dominant stage could justify concentrating AI development effort there. This was guidance on selecting a focus, not a final selection of the activity or solution.

### Buyer acceptance and the boundaries of AI support

**Recording: 17:56–22:48**

- Yijie reported that buyers were uncomfortable with an arrangement in which AI autonomously prepared orders while they only reviewed and approved them. They were concerned about losing awareness of what had been ordered and being unable to answer colleagues' questions.
- Hu treated this as a question about the permitted actions and boundaries of the proposed AI solution.
- Hu suggested reviewing literature on categories of work that AI can support, then comparing these categories with what the buyers would accept AI doing.
- The suggested categories need not match purchasing stages one-to-one. Search and communication were examples of work that can appear in several stages, rather than a final classification.
- A comparison table could set out the literature-based task categories alongside buyer acceptance. The eventual functionality should be limited to the categories buyers accept.
