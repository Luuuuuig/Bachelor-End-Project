## Purpose and continuity

The analytical list starts with **Van Weele's purchasing stages**, then defines the concrete purchasing activities within them using the existing task register and relevant Dennis evidence. An observation is assigned by its recorded action and purpose. Its broad live code is retained as evidence, but is not a lookup key that determines its stage.

The research sequence remains coherent:

1. Exploratory observations and the buyer walkthrough established the AS-IS workflow and detailed task register.
2. Continuous-observation literature informed recording mechanics. Local observations and the pilot informed the practical recording categories and refinements; the method justification was documented on 3 September.
3. Broad families reduced live classification effort during rapid task switching. Detailed Task IDs were deferred to enrichment.
4. Dennis's inclusion broadened the purchasing context. On 17 September, the supervisor asked for the framework to guide the activity list and distinguish similar actions at different stages.
5. This version defines those analytical activities and applies them retrospectively where existing notes support them. It does not claim that Van Weele generated the original seven families or that the complete current list was specified before data collection.

Keep **REQ, CLAR, DEC, PO, CHECK, SEND and OTHER** for current live recording. Historical EXC remains visible and excluded under the current thesis scope. No additional live codes or task-register numbers are introduced. Full activity labels are selected after observation. Retaining seven live families while applying this list afterward is the researcher's implementation choice; the meeting does not establish the supervisor's approval of that exact arrangement.

## Framework and local definitions

The six stages follow [Nevi's purchasing-process explanation](https://nevi.nl/inkoop/inkoopkennis/inkoopproces) and the Van Weele model reproduced by [Bäckstrand et al. (2019), section 3.2, Figure 3](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/123697/Repub_123697_O-A.pdf), from Van Weele (2018, Figure 1.2, p. 9). The first three stages are the tactical part and the later stages the operational part of this model. Employee titles do not determine an observation's stage.

The **activity names, grouping, boundaries and confidence rules below are local operational definitions**, not verbatim categories or a validated measurement instrument supplied by Van Weele. The [author's chapter 2 teaching materials](https://www.arjanvanweele.com/presentations-powerpoint/) place requesting an order confirmation within ordering. This project distinguishes that request from checking and processing a returned confirmation, assigned to monitoring as a stated local rule. Receiving a message does not itself establish buyer work.

A grouped activity name covers its relevant observed sub-actions; it does not assert that every verb or every linked Task ID occurred. Preserve the subtype and exact evidence in the assignment reason. Task IDs are conditional links to the unchanged 31-task register, not newly recorded observations.

## How to enrich a row

1. Retain the source date, actor, row, case, original family or pilot Task IDs, time, volume, interruption/decision flags and note.
2. Read what was done and **why**. Identify the purchasing object and transaction position, using documented same-case context where justified.
3. Select the stage and full activity definition that fit that purpose. Then record any matching existing Task IDs. These judgments use the actual evidence, not an automatic family-to-stage or role-to-stage rule.
4. Record task, stage and activity confidence separately: `C` confident, `P` provisional, `?` unresolved. A broader observed activity can be clear even when its exact register match is provisional. A stage-specific activity must not imply firmer stage knowledge than the evidence supports.
5. Record the assignment reason and apply scope and timing-quality rules independently. A known stage does not make EXC, logistics or contaminated work eligible. An unknown stage does not erase otherwise eligible work.

Keep one source row for an inseparable interval. Use **Combined activities**, name the supported components and count the recorded duration once. Do not split or duplicate its minutes across components or stages. Different activities can occur within one known stage; `MULTI` is reserved for multiple **evidenced** stages. If one component's stage is unknown, retain `UNKNOWN` for the undivided interval and describe what is known.

Use **Unresolved activity** when the recorded action or purpose does not support one list entry. A known stage can coexist with an unresolved specific activity, for example a PO-generated result without evidence of the buyer action. **Not an observed activity** identifies the pilot break and observation gap, which are not buyer-work records.

For future observation, keep the existing live fields. Where feasible, use the short note to capture the object and purpose, such as “clarify required quantity”, “retrieve drawing for defined order”, “compare confirmation with PO” or “compare alternative suppliers”. Do not slow live recording to force a detailed activity; unresolved cases can be checked after the session.

## Analysis and methodological justification

Use scope-eligible, quality-eligible observations for the thesis profile. Report unresolved and provisional coverage, and keep combined durations in their own bucket when comparing specific activities. Do not distribute those minutes across categories or call the frequency of every row a completed-task count. Decisions already recorded on a host episode are not additional timed work.

The amount and difficulty of Arno's work remain the workload construct. Time is one indicator; this classification does not measure difficulty or purchasing quality. The [measurement-method justification](../measurement/Measurement_Method_Justification.md) explains the support from STAMP, WOMBAT and structured observation, and the project-specific adaptations: purchasing categories, seven live families, minute-level intervals, sub-minute tallies, exclusive active timing, post-session task/activity enrichment, and judgement prompts. Those adaptations are explained but have not inherited the literature's instrument validation.

The 17 September suggestion to record overlapping activity durations has **not** been applied retrospectively or adopted by this classification update. Such a change requires a separately dated timing protocol and explicit comparison rules. The accepted main question and four sub-questions are unchanged.

For the thesis, describe this as a later, documented refinement of classification. The early data remain useful for timing, recurrence, family-level patterns and supported activity assignments; their missing detail limits the proportion that can be attributed to specific Van Weele activities.
