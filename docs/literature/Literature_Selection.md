# Literature Selection

This file tracks literature by current likelihood of use in the BEP. The status can change as the research design and selected purchasing use case become more specific.

## Confirmed use

### Workload definition

**Young, M. S., Brookhuis, K. A., Wickens, C. D., & Hancock, P. A. (2015).** *State of science: Mental workload in ergonomics*. *Ergonomics, 58*(1), 1–17. https://doi.org/10.1080/00140139.2014.956151

**Why it is relevant:** Provides a broad review of mental workload in ergonomics and emphasizes that workload is a complex, multidimensional construct rather than a single simple measure. This will support the conceptual definition of workload in the BEP and the distinction between task/process workload and cognitive workload.

### DMAIC / process improvement structure

**The Council for Six Sigma Certification. (2018).** *Six Sigma Green Belt Certification Training Manual*. June 2018 edition. https://www.sixsigmacouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Six-Sigma-Green-Belt-Certification-Training-Manual-CSSC-2018-06b.pdf

**Why it is relevant:** The manual describes DMAIC as a five-phase approach — Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve and Control — for improving an existing process. This directly supports using DMAIC to structure the broader company process-improvement work at Hytech-Pommec. It also distinguishes DMAIC from DMADV, which is more appropriate when a process needs to be newly designed or fundamentally replaced.

## High chance to use

### Multidimensional workload measurement

**Longo, L., Wickens, C. D., Hancock, G., & Hancock, P. A. (2022).** *Human mental workload: A survey and a novel inclusive definition*. *Frontiers in Psychology, 13*, 883321. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.883321

**Why it is relevant:** Reviews definitions and measurement approaches for mental workload and distinguishes self-report, task-performance and physiological measures. This is likely useful for developing a defensible workload-measurement framework that goes beyond only frequency × processing time.

## Possible use when we know more

### Subjective workload measurement / NASA-TLX

**Hart, S. G. (2006).** *NASA-Task Load Index (NASA-TLX); 20 years later*. *Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting, 50*(9), 904–908. https://doi.org/10.1177/154193120605000909

**Why it may become relevant:** NASA-TLX is useful if the BEP formally measures perceived mental workload for selected purchasing tasks. It can capture dimensions such as mental demand, temporal demand, effort and frustration. Whether it is needed depends on whether subjective cognitive workload becomes part of the final measurement and evaluation design.

## Current interpretation for the BEP

At the current stage, workload should not automatically be reduced to one metric. A likely approach is to combine objective process measures — such as task frequency, active processing time, rework and interruptions — with a subjective workload measure only where this adds value for the selected task. The exact operationalization should be finalized after the use case and research design are agreed with the university supervisor.
