## How the weights were set

Swing weighting (Department for Communities and Local Government, 2009; Belton and Stewart, 2002), as planned in [methodology §5.3](../methodology/Phase_1_Current_Methodology.md#53-formal-two-stage-selection-rule) and research action 10:

1. Picture a candidate that scores 1 on every criterion.
2. The criterion whose lift from 1 to 5 matters most gets a swing of 100.
3. Every other criterion gets a swing for how much its lift from 1 to 5 is worth compared with that one.
4. Each swing is divided by the total (370), so the weights add up to 100%.

The swings follow the 1 / 3 / 5 anchors proposed in the brief. Four criteria score only what lies above a Stage A gate, so their range is narrower and their swing lower. The swings were first drafted as a worked example during the analysis on 7 October and adopted by Yijie without change.

## The weights

| Criterion | Lift from 1 to 5 | Swing | Weight |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Observed workload contribution | Under 5% of in-scope buyer time → over 15%, with recorded burden on several days. Reducing buyer workload is the research objective. | 100 | 27.0% |
| Data readiness above the gate minimum | A few hand-collected cases → representative, exportable cases with a traceable correct outcome. Decides how good the prototype and its test can be; gates 1–2 already set the minimum. | 60 | 16.2% |
| AI–task fit | AI needed for one part → AI is the core of the task. The company expects AI in the focal solution; gate 6 already removes tasks a simple rule could do. | 50 | 13.5% |
| Evaluation feasibility | Few cases and little buyer time → many cases with a reference answer and time to test. Needed for SQ5; gate 3 already sets the minimum. | 50 | 13.5% |
| Prototype / technical feasibility | Manual workarounds needed → buildable with available data and tools, no integration. Gate 5 already removes what does not fit the BEP. | 40 | 10.8% |
| Business value beyond counted workload | No effect beyond buyer time → an effect confirmed with examples or figures. Secondary to the workload objective. | 40 | 10.8% |
| Repetition / process standardisation | Rare or varying inputs → many stable cases every day. Frequency is already partly counted under workload. | 30 | 8.1% |
| **Total** | | **370** | **100%** |

In the order of the brief's form: 100, 40, 50, 60, 30, 40, 50.

## What was known when the weights were set

- The workload figures per candidate area (notebook, part 8) and the [evidence matrix](Candidate_Evidence_Matrix_2026-10-07.md), which holds evidence but no scores.
- No candidate had been scored, and no total calculated.
- The swings were set by thinking about each criterion's range, not about any candidate's figures. Having seen the workload figures first is reported as a limitation.
